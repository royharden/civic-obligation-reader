# Limits of the reader

The corpus is original fiction. This reader explains supplied text and never determines legal applicability, compliance or whether an action is advisable. Exact quote and selected-version evidence verifies literal text and date resolution only. A correct quote can accompany a wrong actor or interpretation.

Input is one English JSON packet, at most 128 KiB, with at most twelve total sections and six structured amendments. Supported operations replace text, insert after an exact anchor, add a section or repeal a section. Unstructured, conflicting, failed and retroactive amendments leave persistent visible uncertainty. The reader does not infer a missing amendment.

Deadlines support absolute Gregorian dates, calendar-day counts up to 366 after a stated event, before-event anchors and no stated deadline. Months, business days, holidays, recurring periods, unclear anchors and conflicting figures stay unknown. Supplied trigger dates are assumptions for arithmetic, not factual findings.

Definitions and explicit exceptions resolve at most one direct in-document hop. Missing, repealed, tainted or deeper references remain uncertain. Actor detection and clause boundaries follow the published labelling guide and restricted authoring grammar, not unrestricted natural language understanding.

The six development enactments and expected labels were written by the builder lineage. They cannot establish the preregistered claim. Hidden authoring, blind relabelling, admission and all measured comparisons are pending. Authored replay examples are neither provider responses nor measurements.

All source is currently uninstalled, unbuilt and runtime-unverified. Local storage, clipboard/download behavior, database RLS, email sessions, provider compatibility and cost accounting require the deferred checks before use. A static review is construction acceptance only.

Rulings are personal append-only review events. They become stale when relevant source, dependencies, taint, normalizer/extractor editions or used event dates change. Local and remote histories are separate; remote stores metadata and user notes, never packet text or machine field values. Concurrent ruling branches require reconciliation.
